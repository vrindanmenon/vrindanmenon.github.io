**WORK IN PROGRESS**

What happens when you look at the same mathematical idea in different ways?

The Algebra Lab is an interactive space for exploring algebra through **visual patterns, equations, number lines, graphs, and algebra tiles**.

Instead of moving directly from a question to a formula, the lab gives you different ways to see the relationship and asks you to notice what changes, what stays the same, and how the representations connect.

## One idea. Different ways to see it.

The lab brings four representations together:

### Visual Pattern

Start with a pattern and look for how it grows.

What changes from one step to the next?

Can you predict what comes next?

### Equation

Turn the relationship into an equation.

Change the numbers and see how the relationship responds.

You can also place multiple equations side by side to compare them.

### Number Line

See the relationship along a number line.

Move through the values and notice how the pattern behaves.

### Graph

Plot the relationship and see the pattern take shape.

The graph gives another way to ask the same mathematical question.

### Algebra Tiles

Use tiles to make the repeated structure visible.

The tiles provide a concrete way to look at what the algebra is describing.

---

## The Challenge

The lab is not just a visualiser.

Each challenge begins with a mathematical question and asks you to **predict, test, and explain**.

You can:

* try an answer
* ask for a hint
* change the representation
* experiment with the numbers
* compare relationships
* move between visual and symbolic representations

The aim is to make the connections between representations visible.

---

## The Learning Journey

The Algebra Lab is being developed as a series of small challenges.

Each challenge begins with something that can be **seen or experimented with**, and gradually asks learners to make the underlying mathematical relationship more explicit.

**See → Notice → Predict → Represent → Test → Explain**

---

## What I am exploring

This lab is part of a larger question I am interested in:

**Can we help learners understand algebra before asking them to manipulate algebraic symbols?**

Rather than treating a graph, an equation, a pattern, or a visual model as separate topics, the lab brings them together so that learners can move between representations and see that they are describing the same mathematical relationship.
